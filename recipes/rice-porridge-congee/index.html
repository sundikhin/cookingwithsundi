Sick day / porridge

rice porridge (congee)

Rice porridge (also commonly known as congee) is a staple in my house when we're in the height of sick season. I actually prefer batch cooking this when I'm not sick and freezing it in my 1-cup Souper Cubes, so that I can reach for a single serving when I do get sick. This comes together super easily in a rice cooker, and is fairly hands off.

about 2 hrs makes about 8 cups freezer friendly

ingredients

Tick things off as you go.

you'll need

  • Rice cooker (ideally with a porridge setting)
  • Frying pan

method

  1. fill the rice cooker

    Wash the rice bits and add them to the rice cooker pot. Rice bits are ideal for this recipe because they cook down into a thicker porridge than whole grains of rice. Add your broth. While many purists will use water (that's typically how you get that pure white porridge), when you're sick the added protein can really help give you strength. I go all in with the Costco chicken bone broth I always keep on hand, but regular chicken stock or vegetable broth works fine as well.

    Toss in the smashed garlic and the peeled slices of ginger. Raw ginger is key here. You're never going to find powdered ginger in my recipes.

  2. sear the chicken

    Season the chicken thigh with salt and pepper and pan fry it for 3–5 minutes on each side. You want the skin to render out its fat. A tablespoon of soy sauce in the pan at this stage wouldn't be amiss either, if you have it. The chicken doesn't need to be cooked all the way through; it'll do the bulk of its cooking in the rice cooker. This step just adds a ton of flavor to the broth.

    Add the chicken thigh to the rice cooker.

  3. let it rip

    Set the rice cooker to the porridge setting and let it rip. Once it's done (many rice cookers sing to announce they're finished), give everything a good mix. Tear apart the chicken thigh to spread bits of chicken through the porridge and remove the bone.

  4. top and serve

    Ladle into bowls and top with scallions and a drizzle of soy sauce.

a note from sundi

A good rice cooker is key to how easy and hands off this recipe is. If yours has a porridge setting, opt for that if you can. An Instant Pot can work too, but I've had issues with rice sticking before, so your mileage may vary.
