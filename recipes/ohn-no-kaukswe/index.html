Burmese / noodle soup

ohn no kaukswe

Burmese chicken curry coconut noodle soup. Ohn No Kaukswe is often described as comfort in a bowl by those of us lucky enough to eat it during our childhood. It takes a humble chicken curry and turns it into something wonderfully rich, aromatic, and tangy. There is nothing else quite like the combination of coconut, chickpea, and chicken curry, topped with springy bright aromatics and a squeeze of fresh lime.

about 1½ hrs + marinating serves 4–6 make-ahead curry

you'll need

  • Mortar and pestle (or a food processor)
  • Heavy-bottom pot
  • Tall stock pot
  • Whisk or immersion blender
  • Gloves, for the turmeric

method

  1. marinate the chicken

    The chicken curry can be made ahead of time, and I recommend it: the flavors get deeper as it sits. Cut the chicken thighs into roughly ½-inch cubes and put them in a bowl.

    Peel the ginger and crush it in a mortar and pestle until you get a paste, then squeeze the pulp over the chicken to release the ginger juice. If you don't have a mortar and pestle, slice the ginger into small discs and blitz it in a food processor to a similar paste. This releases the juice too, just not as well, so you may need a bit more ginger to get the same amount.

    Crush and mince the garlic. Add the garlic, fish sauce, 1 tbsp paprika and the turmeric to the bowl with the ginger and chicken. Mix by hand (with gloves! Turmeric is highly potent and will stain your hands and your countertops) or with a silicone spatula until everything is well worked into the chicken. Cover and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes, or overnight.

  2. cook the onions in plenty of oil

    Add enough neutral oil to coat the bottom of a heavy-bottom pot, then add 2–3 more tablespoons. This curry uses a lot of oil on purpose; it's what colors the soup in the end. Skimp on the oil and you'll likely end up with the wrong result.

    Dice the medium onion. When the oil is shimmery (hot enough to simmer the onions, but not fry them), add the onion and cook for 2–3 minutes. Stir in the remaining 2 tbsp paprika and cook for another 10–15 minutes over low to medium heat, taking care not to burn the onions or let them stick. You're there when the onions are cooked through and translucent, and the oil is a deep red.

  3. braise the chicken

    Add the chicken and stir until every piece is coated in the oil, then add about 1 cup of water to loosen it. You're essentially braising the chicken for this kind of curry. Bring it up to a high heat, almost a boil, then turn the heat to low and cover. Cook for 15 more minutes, stirring now and then so nothing sticks. Set the curry aside until the broth is ready.

  4. start the broth

    Pour the 12 cups of chicken broth into a tall stock pot. Quarter the small onion and add it to the broth with the burner still off, then bring the broth to a boil.

    While you wait, whisk the chickpea flour and cold water together in a separate bowl. A regular whisk or an immersion blender both work. You're after smooth, foamy chickpea water, and you want every lump gone now: they're hard to get out once they're in the big pot.

  5. build the soup

    Once the broth is boiling, pour in the chickpea mixture and give it one thorough stir. Cook on medium heat for 10 minutes, then add the coconut milk and coconut cream (or both cans of coconut milk, if that's what you have). Cook for another 5 minutes, until well combined.

    Add the chicken curry, scraping every bit of the curry and paprika-infused oil out of the pot; that's where the flavor and color come from. Cook everything together for another 10 minutes. The curry should season the soup well, but add salt to taste here if it needs it.

  6. cook the noodles

    Any spaghetti or long wheat noodle from the average pasta aisle works here. Thin spaghetti is my favorite for this dish, but regular spaghetti or fettuccine are fine too. Cook the pasta according to the package instructions.

  7. top and serve

    Serve in shallow soup bowls, with a big mound of noodles in the middle and the soup ladled around it. Pile on the toppings: thinly sliced red onion, roughly chopped cilantro, spring onions and a quarter of a hard-boiled egg. Throw some chili powder on top if you're into spice, but definitely don't skip the squeeze of lime.

a note from sundi

The toppings are a choose your own adventure game! Set them all out on the table and let everyone build their own bowl.
